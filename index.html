Motorparts Inventory

Inventory, point of sale and sales tracking for a small motorcycle parts shop in Bulacan. It started as a system for a real shop, and it runs there every day.

What it does

  • Tap a bike model like NMAX or Click to see every part that fits it.
  • A fast counter POS that won't oversell, won't ring up an item at ₱0, and warns when a discount goes below cost.
  • Returns from a receipt: stock goes back, and the refund counts on the day it's given.
  • Daily closing with a bill-by-bill cash count, and reports net of returns.
  • Reads the owner's Google Sheet one way, so they keep the spreadsheet they already use.

How it's built

  • One server file using Node's built-in http and SQLite. No dependencies, no npm install.
  • A plain JavaScript front end with hash routing. No framework, no build step.
  • Phone-first and installable to the home screen, with staff and admin roles.
  • Forced password change on first login, lockout after wrong passwords, and a strict Content Security Policy.

What I learned

To design for the people at the counter: useful buttons, keyboard shortcuts and small quality-of-life touches that save time on every sale. The owner was surprised by what I built. Looking back, the interface feels generic. It was my first POS, and my design has grown a lot since.

The shop's real data is confidential, so the public demo runs on generated sample data. The demo login is in the README.

Off the Clock

My personal site: the things I make, what I do for fun, what I believe in, and where to find me. This portfolio is the professional side, and that site is everything else.

What's on it

  • A photo gallery that loads small images first, and the full size only when you open one.
  • A story timeline, Valorant and basketball walls, and my YouTube and TikTok clips.
  • A small playable teaser for Malolos Rush.

How it's built

  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with Lenis for smooth scrolling.
  • Every word lives in one content file. Drafts marked TODO only show when I preview locally.
  • Photos are resized, colour-graded and stripped of GPS data before they go online.
  • Light and dark themes, and it respects reduced-motion settings.

Why I made it, and what I learned

I started making YouTube videos to share who I am: my hobbies, interests and beliefs. This site is another way to do that, so I wanted it personal, playful and simple. The animations surprised me most. Websites can do far more than what I learned in my first years of college, and it showed me how fast this field moves.

2026 · Group project

The Hive Kiosk

A self-order kiosk for The Hive Cafe, built in C# and WinForms. It was a group project: my teammates built the main POS system, and I built the kiosk on my own.

What it does

  • Seven category tabs, choices for size, temperature and sweetness, and a cart you can edit line by line.
  • English and Filipino, switchable at any point in the order.
  • Sold-out items stay visible but can't be ordered, and checkout checks again.
  • Cash orders get a daily order number to pay at the counter. Card and e-wallet are demo flows, since there's no payment processor.
  • It resets after two minutes idle, so the next guest never inherits an order.

How it's built

  • Every button, tab and tile is drawn by hand with GDI+. No third-party UI library.
  • Orders and availability are saved locally, with a PIN-protected staff screen.
  • Smoke tests check the cart and checkout rules, and render the main screens for layout review.

Looking back

I drew the interface myself partly to understand how rendering actually works. There's still plenty I'd do differently now.
